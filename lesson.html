01 · LESSON OVERVIEW

At the Airport

By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to understand common airport questions, identify key travel vocabulary, and take part in a short check-in interaction using polite requests.

Communicative goal

Ask for information and respond to common questions at an airport.

Language focus

Polite requests with “Could I…?” and airport questions.

Target vocabulary

boarding pass, check-in desk, carry-on bag, departure gate, flight delay, passport, window seat.

Success criteria

Use at least five target expressions and complete a simple role-play.

Suggested lesson flow · 45 minutes

04m
Warm-up & retrievalActivate prior knowledge: What do you need before boarding?
07m
Vocabulary in contextIntroduce the target words using examples and concept checks.
07m
Dialogue & noticingRead a check-in exchange and notice polite language.
07m
Reading for detailRead the flight announcement and find key information.
12m
Communicative role-playPassenger and agent practise, then switch roles.
08m
Assessment & exit ticketComplete the quiz and name one useful phrase.
Teacher preparation:

Open the lesson, review the answer key, prepare pairs and decide whether students will use the online quiz or printed worksheet.

07 · VOCABULARY LAB

Essential airport vocabulary

Read the expression, meaning and example. Then retrieve the word without looking.

boarding pass

cartão de embarque

Keep your boarding pass ready at the gate.
check-in desk

balcão de check-in

Go to the check-in desk to drop off your bag.
carry-on bag

bagagem de mão

You can take one carry-on bag into the cabin.
checked baggage

bagagem despachada

My checked baggage is under the weight limit.
departure gate

portão de embarque

Our departure gate is number 12.
flight delay

atraso de voo

There is a 30-minute flight delay.
passport

passaporte

May I see your passport, please?
window seat

assento na janela

Could I have a window seat, please?
Quick check

Which item do you show when boarding?

Try to recall the word before rereading.
08 · SPEAKING LAB

At the check-in desk

Use the dialogue as a model, then personalise it. Aim for clear, polite and understandable English.

Agent: Good morning. May I see your passport, please?
Passenger: Of course. Here you are.
Agent: Are you checking in any bags?
Passenger: Yes, one suitcase. I also have a carry-on bag.
Agent: Would you prefer an aisle seat or a window seat?
Passenger: A window seat, please, if possible.
Agent: Here is your boarding pass. Your flight departs from Gate 12.

Speaking challenge

Role A · Passenger

Ask for a window seat, say you have one suitcase, and ask when boarding starts.

Role B · Agent

Ask for the passport, ask about bags, offer a seat choice and give a gate number.

Self-assessment

10 · READING LAB

Flight information

Read the announcement. First identify the main message, then scan for details.

FLIGHT 284 — LONDON

Attention, passengers. Flight 284 to London is now boarding at Gate 18. Please have your boarding pass and passport ready. Passengers who need assistance may speak to an airport staff member. The gate will close 15 minutes before departure. Thank you.

1. Where is Flight 284 going?

2. Which documents should passengers have ready?

09 · LISTENING LAB

Listen & notice

This lesson version does not include a recorded audio file. Use the teacher-read script below or record your own reading. Do not present this as a pre-recorded MP3.

Teacher-read script

“Attention, passengers. Flight 284 to London is now boarding at Gate 18. Please have your boarding pass and passport ready. The gate will close 15 minutes before departure.”

Three-pass listening routine

1
GistRead once at natural speed. Learners answer: What is the announcement about?
2
Key detailsRead again. Learners note destination, gate and closing time.
3
Notice languageLearners identify one instruction and one polite expression.

11 · GRAMMAR LAB

Polite requests with “Could I…?”

Use Could I + base verb to make a polite request.

Could I have…?

Could I have a window seat, please?

Could I change…?

Could I change my seat?

Could I put…?

Could I put my bag here?

More direct

“Give me a window seat.” This sounds less polite.

Choose the most polite request.

12 · ASSESSMENT PACK

Exit check

Complete these questions to check the main learning outcomes. This is a formative classroom check, not an official CEFR test.

1. What do you show to board?
2. “May I see your passport?” means…
3. Choose the polite request.
4. A flight delay means…

13 · HOMEWORK & EXTENSION

Take English beyond the lesson

Core task · 10 minutes

Write a 6-line check-in dialogue. Include a passport request, one baggage question, a seat preference and a boarding gate.

Extension · 10–15 minutes

Add a problem: your flight is delayed, your preferred seat is unavailable, or your bag is overweight. Negotiate politely.
